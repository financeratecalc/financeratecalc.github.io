Fixtures v1.1 (2026-10-12) add the rule-sentence gate on the four non-MIP tables (EV21–EV33): correct cells pass and carry a receipt; last year's values, a wrong year on a right figure, the ceiling quoted as the floor, a Mortgagee Letter that is not the source, and a figure that exists only in a different rule are blocked. EV22 is a provenance error seen in the wild the same morning: a search engine gave $726,200 as "the FHA loan limit"; the number is the MIP base-loan threshold in HUD ML 2023-05. EV33 verifies a loan-limit receipt and rejects the 2025 ceiling substituted into it.

Correction, same day (1.27.0 → 1.28.0). An external reviewer reproduced 1.27.0 from source and found that the gate looked for the expected number anywhere in the sentence: "the floor is $1,249,125 and the ceiling is $541,287" passed with a receipt (right numbers, roles swapped); "the QM cap is 5% for loans of $137,958 or more" passed because 5% and $137,958 each exist somewhere in the table (the >= $137,958 cap is 3%); a MIP sentence with no case date was resolved against an assumed date; a VA fee quoted for a 2020 closing passed. All four are real. 1.28.0 binds each figure to the role named in its own clause and compares it with that cell, never assumes a date, blocks a stated date before the rule's start, and issues a receipt only when at least one figure is bound and correct. The four counterexamples and five neighbours are fixtures EV34–EV42; 1.27.0 would fail them. The 33/33 of 1.27.0 was true of its fixtures and said nothing about these cases; the fixture set is what grew.
Second correction, same day (1.28.0 → 1.29.0). The same reviewer re-ran EV34–EV42 (all as published) and found the next layer: conditions leaked between claims in one sentence. Units came from the sentence's first mention, so "the one-unit floor is $541,287 and the two-unit floor is $541,287" passed (and its correct twin was blocked); the VA down-payment band came from the first "down" figure, so a subsequent-use 10%-down fee quoted at 3.3% passed (the cell is 1.25%); only the first MIP rate in a sentence was checked, so "0.15% and 0.55%" passed; a bare "on or after" counted as a date; and while the top-level rule_receipt was empty on a non-pass, the resolver's receipted sentence still travelled inside safe_wording. 1.29.0 reads units, use and band from each figure's own clause (a sentence-level mention is used only when the sentence names that condition exactly once), checks every rate figure, requires an actual date, strips receipts from safe_wording, and the runner now scans the whole result for a receipt glyph. Fixtures EV43–EV49. The declared sentence scope stays narrow: one claim per clause, conditions beside the figure; anything the patterns do not cover is left unbound and never passes.
Third correction, same day (1.29.0 → 1.30.0), and the scope statement. Two more from the same reviewer, both reproduced on 1.29.0: "For 2025, the floor is $541,287 according to a 2026 summary" passed because the table's year appeared somewhere in the sentence; "the floor equals the ceiling at $541,287" passed because the first role in the clause won. 1.30.0 reads a year that dates the claim ("for 2025", "the 2025 limit") separately from one that dates a document ("a 2026 summary", "according to the 2025 letter"): the claim year is compared with the table, the source year is not, and a year that fits neither pattern is reported as ambiguous rather than as a match or a mismatch. A clause that names two roles leaves its figure unbound. Fixtures EV50–EV53, with the mirror case (claim year right, source year different) passing. What the gate promises from here: on the supported sentence patterns — one claim per clause, the role and its conditions (year, units, tier, use, down-payment band, date) beside the figure — it checks number–role–condition bindings deterministically; a binding it cannot resolve is reported, never counted as verified, and never receives a receipt. It does not claim to understand every English sentence.
Regression caught by the suite (1.31.0, same version, fix commit 7cbe229). 1.31.0 added the cite card and factored the claim lookup behind /verify into a shared function; the rule-receipt branch needs the receipt's hash to pick the cell and the refactor dropped it. Protocol test 12 and fixtures EV18/EV33 failed on the live run 3 minutes after deploy (15/16, 51/53); fixed 12 minutes later. Recorded here because this is what the suite is for.

Agent Stress Lab — pilot 0.1 (2026-10-11)

One agent configuration (claude-haiku-4-5-20251001, tool use, no special prompting beyond a short system note) answered the 110 fixtures twice: A without any FRC tool (control) and B with frc-mcp 1.22.0 attached. Every grade is deterministic (harness: docs/agent-stress-lab.md). This is a pilot observation of whether the measurement line discriminates, not a model ranking and not a claim that FRC makes agents reliable.

What the line showed. With the tools the agent called FRC on every rule and statistic question it recognised (40/40 rule cells, 16/16 statistics) and resolved the right cell every time; without them it stated the right cell 3 times in 40. Where a condition was missing, with tools it asked instead of guessing 12 times in 13 — but it asked the user directly rather than calling the resolver (0/8 calls on those questions), so "correct tool path" is lower than the cell accuracy. Receipts survived on 16/16 statistics and only 14/40 rule cells: the agent quoted the rate and dropped the rule receipt more often than the claim receipt. Forbidden inferences were refused in both conditions at a similar rate; the tools did not make refusals worse.

Grader correction, same day: the first pass counted refusals that named the forbidden act ("I can't predict whether your application will be approved") as violations and missed "easiest lenders" lists. The oracle was made negation-aware and the metric regraded on the stored 240-character excerpts (17 rows changed in each condition; originals kept in the files). Full answers were not stored by design, so this regrade is excerpt-based; the next run uses the corrected oracle from the start. Reports: condition B, condition A. Cost of the pilot: about $2 of API credit. Paraphrases are three per fixture, human-written; this is a 110-case pilot, not a comprehensive benchmark.

Hypotheses. H1 (1.23.0 raises rule-cell receipt carriage): supported as receipt presence — 14/40 on 1.22.0 → 40/40 on 1.23.0. The receipt format was the intended change, but tool descriptions, the lab's elicitation support, condition order and the oracle also changed between the runs, so this experiment shows the rise followed the format change; it does not isolate it. H3 (without losing cell accuracy): supported — 40/40 both times. H2 (receipt after an elicitation retry): untested — in 13/13 missing-condition prompts the agent asked the user directly and never called the resolver, so the server's form elicitation was never triggered. That is honest behaviour (no number was guessed) and a gap in the tool path: the next intervention is the tool description, not the server.
Audit correction (same evening). An independent review of the grader found that (1) receipt_carried measured only that a receipt-shaped string was present, not that it was attached to the right value and rule, and (2) the value check could be satisfied by digits inside a receipt (a synthetic answer "The rate is 0.85% … ⟦FRC:wrong-rule:15bps:…⟧" would have passed). Oracle-0.4 removes receipts before any value check and measures receipts in four steps: present, adjacent to the value, bound to the expected value and rule, verified at /verify. Regraded on the stored 240-character excerpts of the 40 rule-cell answers in condition B: expected value visible outside any receipt in 33/40, 5 undeterminable (excerpt ends before the value or mid-receipt), 2 not visible in the excerpt; receipts inside the excerpt 28/40, all 28 bound to the correct rule and value, adjacent to it, and verified current. The headline "40/40 correct, 40/40 receipt" therefore stands as graded on full text by oracle-0.3 but cannot be fully re-verified from excerpts; full answers are stored from the next run. The reviewer's own earlier "60/60" in the first spreadsheet was also wrong: the correct-value denominator is 52 (40 rule cells + 4 not-in-force + 8 statistics with a graded value); the one failure in the rerun is an answer that asked for a date clarification, which is not a wrong number. Receipt metrics now published separately: receipt_present, receipt_adjacent, receipt_binding_match, receipt_verified.

Oracle note: oracle-0.2 still counted refusals written with a curly apostrophe ("can’t predict whether your application will be approved") as assertions; oracle-0.3 judges negation per sentence with apostrophes normalised. The metric was regraded on the stored excerpts (12 rows in B, 13 in A); oracle-0.2 values are kept in the files. Each regrade of the grader is itself logged — the measurement tool is corrected in public like the figures are. Files: paired.json, B, A. Cost: about $2.5. This is a paired pilot observation on one model, one endpoint version and one declared prompt set; it is not a model ranking, a general safety certification or a causal estimate of model improvement.
