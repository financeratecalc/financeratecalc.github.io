ECOA Code 18 · ECOA-18

"Excessive obligations in relation to income"

By Ziya Y. · 23 Years Banking · FinanceRateCalc

Plain meaning: the lender added up your monthly debt payments (the new mortgage included), divided by your gross monthly income, and the result was above the ceiling that lender applies. In mortgage language this is a debt-to-income (DTI) denial. The wording is Regulation B’s: “Excessive obligations in relation to income” is one of the reasons on the sample adverse-action notice in Appendix C to 12 CFR 1002 (Form C-1). The number 18 is not in the regulation — it comes from the numbered reason-code lists lenders print on their standard denial forms, which most of the industry shares — so a notice may show the number, the wording, or both. In the federal HMDA record the same reason is filed as denial reason 1, “debt-to-income ratio”.

How often this reason is given

In the complete 2025 federal record, among the 100 largest FHA lenders, the median lender cited debt-to-income on 40.8% of its denials — the most-cited reason, ahead of credit history (25.9%) and collateral (10.8%). The spread between lenders is wide: at one end a lender cited DTI on 88.4% of its denials, at the other on a small minority. Agency rule or lender overlay?

This is the question that decides what to do next, and the answer depends on how your file was underwritten.

Manually underwritten FHA files have published ceilings in HUD Handbook 4000.1: 31% front / 43% back with no compensating factor; 37/47 with one (documented reserves, a minimal payment increase, or residual income); 40/40 with no discretionary debt; 40/50 with two compensating factors. Below a 580 score, or with no score, 31/43 is the limit. A manual file denied above those lines is an agency rule, not an overlay.

Files scored by TOTAL (the automated FHA scorecard) are different: the handbook sets no fixed DTI number for an Accept. The numbers lenders quote for “FHA max DTI” are their own thresholds layered on top of the scorecard — overlays — and they differ from lender to lender. That is why a file can be denied for code 18 at one lender and accepted at another with the same income and debts. The 2025 record shows the result: denial rates across the 100 largest FHA lenders ran from 1.8% to 78.7% in the same programme and the same year, and applicant mix explains only part of it (why rates differ →).

What to ask for

The adverse-action notice must state the principal reason; it need not state the threshold. Ask the lender, in writing, for the DTI they calculated, which debts they counted (deferred student loans, co-signed debts, payments ending within ten months and alimony are the usual disputes), and whether the file was a TOTAL Accept, a TOTAL Refer, or a manual underwrite. With those three answers you know whether the limit was HUD’s or the lender’s, and whether a recalculation — paying down a revolving balance before closing, documenting income that was left out, or removing a debt that should not have been counted — would have changed the result.

The part almost nobody publishes free

Prices and rates are widely reported. Whether a lender says yes is not. In the complete 2025 federal record, denial rates across the 100 largest FHA lenders ran from 1.8% to 78.7% — same programme, same year.

And it is not simply who applies where: standardizing on state, loan amount, income, debt-to-income and loan-to-value, applicant mix explains only a 2.7× range in expected outcomes.
