FinanceRateCalc · one figure, followed to the end

One figure. Every check, and who made it.

On 25 November an AI system made a decision that relied on this figure. Below is every link between the government report the figure came from and that decision. Each link was made, or checked, by a different party. Where your browser can check a link itself, it does so as the page loads.

6.42FHA serious delinquency rate, August 2026, as first printed in HUD’s loan performance report (percent of FHA-insured loans, not seasonally adjusted).

HUD’s September report later printed August as 6.48, without a revision mark. The receipt for 6.42 stays current because it records what the August report printed, and it carries the later value too. That difference is the reason this chain exists.

And when the figure changes after the decision?

A second case, on a test copy of the same figure, follows the harder question: the decision relied on revision 1, and the publisher issued a correction afterwards. Was the decision right at the time it was made? The correction carries the hash of a Bitcoin block mined after the one that anchors the decision, so it cannot be older than that block. Tiago Pinto found that block 143 blocks after 977556 on his own node. The order follows from the bytes and the block heights, with no one’s clock involved.

The test case, with what it establishes and what it does not

Every link above is described in an Internet-Draft and in the public repository. A receipt shows who published a figure and whether a quote still matches it. It never shows that the figure is right. Try changing a digit on the home page.
