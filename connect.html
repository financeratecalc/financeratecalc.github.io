Add FinanceRateCalc to the assistant you already use

The assistant then answers US mortgage questions from the federal record, with a receipt next to each number that you or your reader can check. What sets this connector apart from other mortgage data sources: the HMDA figures are for 2025, they go down to the individual lender, official monthly reports from HUD, Ginnie Mae and the SBA are included as printed, and every figure can be verified after the fact.

What to ask first

“What share of FHA mortgage applications were denied in Ohio in 2025, and how does that compare nationally? Keep the receipts.”

The answer should carry two figures, each followed by a short code in double brackets. Paste either code into the verify page: it says whether the figure is still current.

“What is the annual FHA mortgage insurance premium on a 30-year loan?”

The assistant should ask you for the loan amount, the loan-to-value ratio and the case number date instead of giving a rate. The premium table has eleven cells; the connector refuses to guess one.

What it will not do. It returns historical aggregates from public records. It does not say whether a particular person will be approved, does not recommend or rank lenders, and gives no legal, credit or underwriting advice. A lender's denial rate reflects who applied as well as how the lender decides. One tool, a batch screen of lenders, returns a three-row preview without a licence; everything else is free and needs no sign-in. The connector asks for no personal data.
