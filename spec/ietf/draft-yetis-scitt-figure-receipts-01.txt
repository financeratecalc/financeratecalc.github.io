  Figure Statements: A SCITT Profile for Published Figures Given to AI
                                 Agents
                  draft-yetis-scitt-figure-receipts-01

Abstract

   Profiles of SCITT for AI agents record what an agent did: which tool
   it called, under whose authority, and what bytes came back.  They
   state, correctly, that they do not show whether those bytes were
   true.  This document profiles the layer underneath: a Signed
   Statement, issued by the publisher of a figure, that binds the figure
   to its source, period and population, and that is superseded when the
   publisher corrects the figure or when the original source prints a
   different value in a later edition.  It also defines a reference to
   such a statement that an agent-side record can carry, and a short
   token that travels with the figure through prose, so that a later
   reader can find the statement and learn whether the figure is still
   the one published.  A superseding statement can carry the hash of a
   recent block of a public chain, which gives it a lower bound in time
   of its own, so that a record anchored earlier on the agent's side can
   be shown to precede the correction.

   The profile makes a narrow claim.  It shows that a figure is the one
   a named publisher published, from a named source, and whether it has
   changed since.  It does not show that the figure is correct.

1.  Introduction

   An AI agent that answers a question with a statistic usually obtained
   the statistic from somewhere: a tool, a retrieved page, a data
   service.  Several current proposals make the agent's side of this
   exchange accountable.  A SCITT profile for agent action receipts
   [I-D.noa-scitt-ai-agent-receipt] records each governed action in a
   signed, hash-chained record.  An execution receipt [I-D.zambo-aer1]
   commits to the exact bytes a tool returned.  A measurement capsule



Yetis                      Expires 7 June 2027                  [Page 2]

Internet-Draft              Figure Statements              December 2026


   [I-D.templeman-scitt-measurement-capsule] records what a third party
   observed against what a subject declared.

   Each of these is explicit about its boundary.  A receipt for an
   action shows that a record was issued by a key and has not been
   altered; it does not show that the agent's inputs were true.  A
   commitment to output bytes shows that the bytes match; it says
   nothing about the value inside them.  The SCITT architecture itself
   is as plain: registering a Statement only proves that it was produced
   by an Issuer [RFC9943].

   So a chain of agent receipts can be complete and verifiable while the
   number at the end of it is stale.  This happens in practice in two
   ways that the agent cannot see:

   *  The publisher corrects a figure.  Copies of the earlier value
      persist in caches, datasets and model outputs.

   *  The original source restates a figure.  Official statistical
      reports print the previous period beside the current one, and the
      restated value sometimes differs from the value first printed,
      without any mark.  Section 16 reports five such cases found in two
      monthly reports of one agency.

   This document defines a Figure Statement: a SCITT Signed Statement
   whose Issuer is the publisher of the figure and whose Subject is the
   figure.  It is the publisher's half of the record.  An agent-side
   receipt can then commit to output that carries a reference to a
   Figure Statement, and a Relying Party can check both halves.

   The reference by which an agent-side record points at a
   Figure Statement (Section 5) follows the requirements proposed in
   [I-D.gruszka-scitt-statement-identification], and was added in this
   revision in response to review on the SCITT mailing list.  So were
   the state "issued in error" (Section 5), the lower bound in time for
   a superseding statement (Section 6) and the requirement that a
   verification result say where it was obtained (Section 8.1).  Each
   came from an implementation that was checked by someone other than
   its author.

   Defining payload formats is outside the charter of the SCITT Working
   Group.  This document is offered as an individual profile, in the
   same way as the agent profiles it complements.

2.  Terminology

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT" and "MAY" in
   this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 [RFC2119]
   [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all capitals, as shown
   here.

   Artifact, Statement, Signed Statement, Issuer, Subject, Transparency
   Service, Receipt, Transparent Statement and Relying Party are used as
   defined in [RFC9943].  In this document "Receipt" always has that
   meaning.

   Figure:  A single published value together with what it measures: a
      quantity, a period, a population and a source.

   Publisher:  The party that publishes a Figure and issues the
      Figure Statement for it.  The Publisher may have computed the
      Figure or may be restating a Figure printed by someone else.

   Computed Figure:  A Figure the Publisher derived from a dataset by a
      stated method.

   Restated Figure:  A Figure the Publisher copied, without computation,
      from a document issued by another party (the Source).

   Edition:  One dated issue of a recurring Source document.

   Figure Statement:  The Statement defined in Section 3.

   Figure Reference:  The structured reference to one Figure Statement
      defined in Section 5.

   Figure Token:  The short textual pointer defined in Section 7.

3.  The Figure Statement

   A Figure Statement is a JSON object.  Before hashing or signing it is
   serialized with the JSON Canonicalization Scheme [RFC8785].  It has
   the following members.

   id (string, REQUIRED):  An identifier for the Figure, unique within
      the Publisher, made of lowercase letters, digits and hyphens.  It
      does not change when the value changes.

   value (string, REQUIRED):  The Figure exactly as it is displayed,
      including any unit sign and digit grouping.

   kind (string, REQUIRED):  "computed" or "restated".



Yetis                      Expires 7 June 2027                  [Page 4]

Internet-Draft              Figure Statements              December 2026


   claim (object, REQUIRED):  The machine form of the Figure: at least a
      numeric value, a metric name and a period.  This is the object the
      Figure Token's digest is taken over (Section 7).

   source (object, REQUIRED):  For a Restated Figure: the title of the
      Source document, its Edition, the SHA-256 digest of the document
      as retrieved, the table and the row or cell the value was copied
      from, and any exclusion the Source states for that table.

      For a Computed Figure: the dataset and its version or digest, the
      definition of the population, the numerator and the denominator as
      counts where they exist, and a reference to the method.

   issued (string, REQUIRED):  The time of issue, as an [RFC3339]
      timestamp.

   supersedes (string, OPTIONAL):  The Figure Token of the
      Figure Statement this one replaces.

   source_revision (object, OPTIONAL):  For a Restated Figure only: the
      value the Source printed for the same period in a later Edition,
      and that Edition.  See Section 9.

   limits (array of strings, OPTIONAL):  Statements the Figure does not
      support, in the Publisher's words; for example that an aggregate
      rate is not a probability for an individual.

   not_before (object, OPTIONAL):  The identifier of a block of a public
      chain that the Publisher observed before issuing the statement:
      "chain", a name for the chain, and "block_hash", the block's hash.
      It MAY also give the height the Publisher was told; that value is
      informative.  See Section 6.

   A Publisher MUST NOT issue a Figure Statement of kind "restated" for
   a value it has adjusted, rounded differently from the Source, or
   derived from several cells.

   A Publisher that builds Figure Statements on request MUST NOT sign
   one when any input it is built from could not be read.  It answers
   that no statement is available and that the request can be repeated.
   A statement built from incomplete inputs is a valid Signed Statement
   from the right key, and nothing in it shows that a member is missing;
   Section 16 reports such a case.

4.  Use with SCITT

   A Figure Statement is carried as the payload of a Signed Statement
   [RFC9943], a COSE_Sign1 object [RFC9052], with these requirements in
   addition to those of the architecture.

   *  The Issuer (CWT Claims "iss") MUST be an HTTPS URI under the
      Publisher's control.

   *  The Subject (CWT Claims "sub") MUST be an HTTPS URI that contains
      the Figure's id and at which the current Figure Statement can be
      retrieved.

   *  The content type MUST be "application/json".  The payload MUST be
      the canonical serialization.

   *  Member names in the payload MUST be ASCII.  The ordering rule of
      [RFC8785] sorts names by UTF-16 code units, and implementations
      that sort by code points disagree for some non-ASCII names; values
      are not affected.

   When a Figure changes, for any of the reasons in Section 9, the
   Publisher MUST issue a new Signed Statement with the same Issuer and
   Subject, and SHOULD register it with every Transparency Service that
   holds the earlier one.  The earlier Signed Statement is not
   withdrawn.  This follows the architecture's guidance for an Issuer
   whose view of an Artifact changes.

   Registration matters here for a specific reason.  A Publisher that
   corrects a Figure quietly, replacing the value and leaving no trace,
   defeats every downstream check.  A Transparency Service that holds
   both statements, with Receipts, dates the change by a log the
   Publisher does not control.

5.  The Figure Reference

   A record made on the agent's side needs to say which
   Figure Statement, and which revision of it, the agent relied on.  A
   Figure Reference says this with three values.

   iss:  The Issuer of the Signed Statement.

   sub:  The Subject of the Signed Statement.

   digest:  The SHA-256 digest of the COSE Sig_structure of the Signed






Yetis                      Expires 7 June 2027                  [Page 6]

Internet-Draft              Figure Statements              December 2026


      Statement, that is, of the CBOR encoding of ["Signature1",
      body_protected, external_aad, payload] as defined in [RFC9052],
      with an empty external_aad and the payload attached.  This is the
      "ToBeSigned" scheme of
      [I-D.gruszka-scitt-statement-identification].

   A Figure Reference MUST name the rule by which its digest was
   computed, together with the hash algorithm, for example as {"alg":
   "sha-256", "scheme": "ToBeSigned", "value": ...}. Several rules in
   current use select the same statements and give different values: the
   rule above, an identifier over the protected header and payload with
   a different encoding [I-D.moore-scitt-ciris-ceg], and a digest over
   the transmitted bytes [I-D.wright-verdict-record].  Without the name,
   two correct implementations fail to find each other's statements.

   The digest is taken over the to-be-signed structure and not over the
   transmitted bytes for a practical reason.  Signature algorithms in
   common use are randomized, so the same statement, signed or fetched
   twice, can differ in its signature bytes; and Receipts added to the
   unprotected header change the transmitted bytes without changing the
   statement.  Neither changes this digest.  The payload alone would not
   do either, because it omits the Issuer and Subject.

   A Figure Reference is carried outside the content it describes: in a
   member of the agent-side record, or in a structured field of a tool
   result, so that a verifier can read it without parsing output text or
   the statement's payload.  How a particular record format carries the
   three values is for that format to define.  An optional member
   listing one Figure Reference for each source a decision used, as
   proposed for [I-D.dobson-ai-decision-receipt], is sufficient.

   A Publisher MUST make public, for each Figure, the digests of every
   Figure Statement it has issued for it, in order of issue, and MUST
   NOT remove an entry.  Given a Figure Reference, a verifier reports
   one of:

   current:  The digest is that of the statement now in force for the
      Subject.

   superseded:  The digest is in the Publisher's list for the Subject
      but is not the last entry.  The verifier reports the statement now
      in force.

   issued in error:  The digest is in the Publisher's list for the







Yetis                      Expires 7 June 2027                  [Page 7]

Internet-Draft              Figure Statements              December 2026


      Subject, marked as issued in error: the Publisher signed and
      served the statement, and it was never the statement in force.
      The verifier reports the statement now in force and any note the
      Publisher gives, and MUST NOT treat the statement as an earlier
      one that was in force.

   unrecognized:  The digest is not in the list for the Subject.

   A statement issued in error verifies under the Publisher's key, and
   only a rule that reads the Publisher's list can tell it from a
   statement in force.  A Publisher that finds it has served such a
   statement MUST add its digest to the list, marked, with the date and
   the cause.  It MUST NOT leave it out: a statement signed by the
   Publisher and answered "unrecognized" by the Publisher looks like a
   forgery to anyone who holds it.

   Each version of the list SHOULD carry a sequence number and the
   digest of the version before it, so that a result can name the
   version it read and a third party can repeat the check on the same
   bytes.

   These states are a matter of time, and neither party can settle them
   alone.  If the agent-side record is not anchored in time by something
   the agent's operator does not control, a later correction by the
   Publisher cannot be told from what the agent saw.  If the Publisher's
   list is not dated by something the Publisher does not control, a
   digest the agent anchored cannot be told from one the Publisher never
   issued.  Registration of each Figure Statement with a Transparency
   Service provides the second; until then a Publisher SHOULD at least
   commit its list to a public log it does not operate.

6.  Ordering a Reference and a Correction

   A Relying Party often needs to know more than whether a statement is
   now superseded.  It needs to know whether the agent relied on it
   before it was superseded.  That is a question about the order of two
   records made by two parties.

   Most time evidence available today is an upper bound: an anchor in a
   public chain shows that bytes existed no later than a given block,
   and an entry in a log shows that bytes existed no later than the
   log's time for it.  Two upper bounds do not order two records,
   whatever the interval between them.








Yetis                      Expires 7 June 2027                  [Page 8]

Internet-Draft              Figure Statements              December 2026


   A superseding Figure Statement that carries "not_before" has a lower
   bound of its own.  It cannot have been made before the named block
   was mined.  If an agent-side record is anchored in the same chain at
   a height at or below that block's height, the agent-side record
   existed before the superseding statement was made.  The order then
   follows from the bytes and the heights, with no clock on either side.

   A verifier using this rule:

   *  MUST take the height of the named block from a chain it has
      validated itself, by looking the hash up, and not from the
      statement or from a third party's report of the chain.

   *  MUST compare heights and not block times.  A block's time is
      declared by the party that produced it.

   *  Learns nothing about statements that do not carry "not_before".  A
      revision issued without it stays unordered against the agent-side
      record, even if a later revision carries it.

   A Publisher need not validate a chain itself, and usually reads a
   block hash from a service.  A wrong hash loses the Publisher the
   bound; it cannot give the Publisher an earlier or a later one,
   because a hash that is not in the chain bounds nothing.

   Registration of both records in one Transparency Service gives an
   order of registrations, not of the records.  It orders the records
   only under a rule that a superseding statement takes effect from its
   registration.  A Publisher that registers its Figure Statements and
   relies on this MUST NOT serve a superseding statement, or answer
   "superseded" for the statement it replaces, before the superseding
   statement is registered.

7.  The Figure Token

   A Signed Statement, and a Figure Reference, are too large to sit
   beside a number in a sentence.  The Figure Token is a short pointer
   that can.  It serves a human reader and text that has left its
   structured carrier; where a structured field is available, the
   Figure Reference is the thing to carry.











Yetis                      Expires 7 June 2027                  [Page 9]

Internet-Draft              Figure Statements              December 2026


   token   = open prefix ":" id ":" value ":" digest
             [ ":" channel ] close
   open    = %xE2.9F.A6   ; U+27E6, UTF-8 encoded
   close   = %xE2.9F.A7   ; U+27E7, UTF-8 encoded
   prefix  = 2*8ALPHA     ; chosen by the Publisher
   id      = 1*( %x61-7A / DIGIT / "-" )
   value   = 1*( %x21-39 / %x3B-7E )
             ; as displayed; no ":" and no space
   digest  = 8HEXDIG      ; lowercase
   channel = ALPHA 4DIGIT ; where and when this copy was issued

   The digest is the first eight hexadecimal characters of the SHA-256
   digest of the canonical serialization of the "claim" member.  The
   brackets were chosen because they do not occur in ordinary prose or
   in JSON syntax; a verifier SHOULD also accept a token whose brackets
   were lost in copying.

   The token is a pointer, not a proof.  Thirty-two bits do not resist a
   deliberate collision, and nothing in the token is signed.  A verifier
   MUST resolve the token to the Figure Statement and compare the full
   digest before reporting any state.

   A Publisher makes the following discoverable at a well-known location
   on the Issuer's origin: the prefix it uses, a URI template from id to
   Figure Statement, the digest rule and any earlier rule with its end
   date, and a list of every digest ever issued for each id.

8.  Verification

   Given a token, a verifier retrieves the current Figure Statement for
   the id and reports exactly one of these states.

   current:  The digest matches the current statement and the value in
      the token equals its "value" member.

   superseded:  The digest does not match the current statement but
      appears in the Publisher's list of issued digests for that id.
      The Figure was changed after this token was issued.  The verifier
      reports the current value.

   altered:  The digest matches the current statement but the value in
      the token does not.  The number beside a genuine reference was
      changed.

   unrecognized:  The id is unknown, or the digest was never issued for
      it.





Yetis                      Expires 7 June 2027                 [Page 10]

Internet-Draft              Figure Statements              December 2026


   Independently of the state, if the current statement has a
   "source_revision" member the verifier MUST report it.  A token can be
   "current", because the Publisher's record of the first printing is
   unchanged, while the Source has since printed another value.

   Verification needs only the Publisher's public files.  A verifier
   SHOULD be able to run where the reader is, without sending the token
   to the Publisher (see Section 13).

8.1.  Where a Result Was Obtained

   What a verifier receives from a Publisher's address can depend on
   where the request was made and what the client looked like.  Caches
   can serve an earlier answer, and services in front of a Publisher can
   refuse some clients or alter what they serve.  None of this is an
   attack, and none of it is visible in a result that says only
   "current" or "not current".  Two parties checking the same reference
   at the same moment can then both be right and disagree.

   A verification result that is recorded or passed to another party
   SHOULD state the time it was obtained, the address asked, where the
   request was made from as far as the verifier can tell, the client
   used, and, where the check was made by software, the release of that
   software.  A result obtained from the Publisher's published list by
   digest, rather than from a live answer, SHOULD name the version of
   the list it read.

9.  Changes to a Figure

   Three different events change what a careful reader should be told.
   They are kept apart because they have different authors.

   1.  The Publisher corrects its own Figure.  A new Figure Statement is
       issued with "supersedes".  Earlier tokens verify as "superseded".

   2.  The Source issues a marked revision.  For a Restated Figure the
       Publisher issues a statement for the revised value, citing the
       revising Edition, and adds "source_revision" to the statement for
       the first printing.

   3.  The Source prints a different value for the same period in a
       later Edition without marking it.  The Publisher treats it
       exactly as case 2.  The absence of a mark is recorded in the
       "source_revision" member, since the reader of the later Edition
       has no other way to learn that the value moved.






Yetis                      Expires 7 June 2027                 [Page 11]

Internet-Draft              Figure Statements              December 2026


   In cases 2 and 3 the statement for the first printing remains true as
   a record of what that Edition printed, and its token remains
   "current".  A Publisher of Restated Figures therefore needs to read
   each new Edition against the last, for the periods both contain,
   before issuing statements for the new one.

10.  Use with Agent Receipts and Tool Protocols

   A tool that returns a Figure to an agent SHOULD return the
   Figure Reference, and MAY return the Figure Token, in a structured
   field of the result, in addition to any text.  Measurement reported
   in Section 16 found that a token placed in prose is dropped by a
   language model in nearly every restatement, so the text is not a
   reliable carrier.

   Where an agent-side record commits to the bytes of a tool result, as
   in [I-D.zambo-aer1] and [I-D.noa-scitt-ai-agent-receipt], and those
   bytes contain Figure References, the two records compose without
   either format changing.  Composition is stronger where the agent-side
   record carries the references in a member of its own (Section 5): a
   verifier then learns which statements were relied on without parsing
   the output at all.  A Relying Party verifies the agent-side record to
   learn what was returned and when, and then checks each reference to
   learn whether that statement was current, superseded or never issued.
   Neither check implies the other.

   An agent or its operator MAY re-verify tokens at the time an answer
   is shown, not only at the time the tool was called.  For long-running
   work and for stored memory the two times differ, and a Figure can be
   superseded between them.

11.  What a Figure Statement Does Not Establish

   *  That the Figure is correct.  A Publisher can compute wrongly, and
      a Source can print a wrong value.  The statement makes the
      Publisher accountable for what it published; it does not check it.

   *  That the population or the denominator is the right one for a
      reader's question.  They are stated so that the reader can decide.

   *  That a Restated Figure is the Source's latest value.  It is the
      value in the named Edition.

   *  Anything about a sentence built around the Figure.  A current
      token beside a number does not license a causal claim, a forecast
      or a statement about an individual.

   *  That the party presenting a token is the Publisher or is endorsed
      by it.

12.  Security Considerations

   The considerations of [RFC9943] apply.  The following are specific to
   this profile.

   Short digest.  The eight-character digest is an index.  An attacker
   can construct a different claim object with the same eight
   characters.  This yields nothing unless a verifier trusts the token
   alone, which Section 7 forbids.

   Borrowed tokens.  A genuine token can be placed beside an unrelated
   number or an unrelated sentence.  The "altered" state detects a
   changed value.  It does not detect a changed meaning; see Section 11.

   A list kept only by the Publisher.  The list of issued digests
   required by Section 5 is evidence only to the extent that its history
   cannot be rewritten.  A Publisher that hosts it alone can remove an
   entry and make a superseded statement look as if it was never issued.

   Equivocation by the Publisher.  Without registration, a Publisher can
   serve different statements to different readers or rewrite its list
   of issued digests.  Registration with a Transparency Service, and
   Receipts, are what make the history checkable.

   Statements built from incomplete inputs.  A Publisher that assembles
   statements on request can sign one from which a member is missing,
   for example when the read of one input fails.  Such a statement
   verifies.  Section 3 forbids issuing it, and Section 5 requires that
   one issued anyway be listed as issued in error; the list is how a
   reader can tell.

   Key compromise and rotation.  One key signs every statement of a
   Publisher.  A Publisher SHOULD publish its key at a stable address on
   the Issuer's origin together with the dates from which it is used,
   and when it replaces a key, SHOULD list the old key with the date it
   stopped being used.  If a key is compromised, statements signed with
   it after the date of compromise cannot be told from forgeries by
   their signature alone; the list of issued digests, if its history is
   held outside the Publisher's control, is then the only way to tell
   them apart.  This is a further reason for registration.

   Source documents.  The digest of a Source document shows which bytes
   the Publisher read.  If the Source replaces a document under the same
   name, the digest no longer matches the document a reader retrieves.
   Publishers SHOULD record a public archive capture of the bytes they
   read.

13.  Privacy Considerations

   A verification request tells the Publisher that someone holds a given
   Figure, and when.  A Publisher that offers a verification service
   SHOULD store no network address and no full user-agent string with
   such requests, and SHOULD publish what it does store.  Because
   verification needs only public files, a verifier can run in the
   reader's own software and make no request that names the token.

   A Figure Statement describes published aggregates.  It MUST NOT
   contain information about an identifiable person.

14.  Related Work

   A working paper of the Bank for International Settlements
   [BIS-WP1374] describes a prototype that binds statistical datasets
   exchanged in SDMX to their source: each dataset, and optionally each
   time series, is hashed; batches are summarized in a Merkle root
   anchored in a public ledger; and a signed credential travels in the
   file.  It works at the level of the dataset and the series.  It lists
   the anchoring of individual observations and verification by AI
   agents as future work, and does not describe how a corrected dataset
   is related to the one it replaces.  This profile works at the level
   of one figure, and is mainly about that relation.  The two are
   complementary: a dataset-level anchor can show which file a figure
   was read from, and a Figure Statement can say what the figure in it
   was and whether it has since changed.

   A register of a publisher's disclosed figures, committed together as
   of a date, chained to the previous set and anchored, answers a
   different question from a Figure Statement: what the publisher had
   disclosed on a date, and what it has withdrawn since.  A register-
   level profile is in preparation by T.  Meister; its entries can be
   cited by a Figure Reference once the reference names the register's
   digest rule.

   Height-based time bounds from a public chain, and verification
   against a chain the verifier has validated, are specified in
   [I-D.fassbender-scitt-time-anchor]; Section 6 uses them and does not
   define them again.

   that wrote neither the agent-side record nor the Figure Statement,

16.  Implementation Status

   This section is to be removed before publication as an RFC.  It
   follows [RFC7942].

   One implementation exists, operated by the author at
   financeratecalc.com.  As of the date of this document:

   *  716 Figures carry tokens: figures computed from the public United
      States Home Mortgage Disclosure Act record, and figures restated
      from monthly reports of the Department of Housing and Urban
      Development, Ginnie Mae and the Small Business Administration.

   *  The claim object, digest rule, token syntax, verification states
      and the well-known manifest are implemented.  Some member and
      state names differ from this document, which generalizes them (the
      implementation says "stale" where this document says "superseded",
      and "display_value" for "value").  A verification service, an in-
      browser verifier that uses only the public files, and a
      conformance suite that another publisher can run against its own
      site are public.

   *  A tool server returns each Figure with its token to AI agents.

   *  Since revision -00: each of the 716 Figures is served as a
      COSE_Sign1 Signed Statement (ES256) with the Issuer and Subject in
      the CWT Claims header and the canonical Figure Statement as
      payload, on the Issuer's own origin.  A Figure Reference that
      names its digest rule is returned with it, and a check answers
      current, superseded, issued in error or unrecognized for a given
      digest, stating the time and address of the check.  The list of
      issued digests is a file in the publisher's public repository with
      a sequence number and the digest of the previous version; it is
      signed and recorded in a public transparency log of another kind
      each time it changes, and it begins on 25 November 2026, so a
      statement replaced before that date cannot be recognized.






Yetis                      Expires 7 June 2027                 [Page 15]

Internet-Draft              Figure Statements              December 2026


   *  Verification of the signatures and recomputation of the digests
      were repeated with a CBOR library and a cryptographic library that
      share no code with the implementation.  Because the signatures are
      randomized, the implementation's own test fetches a statement
      twice and requires different bytes and the same digest.

   *  Not yet implemented: registration of Signed Statements with a
      SCITT Transparency Service, and therefore Receipts as defined in
      [RFC9943].  The tool server does not yet return the
      Figure Reference in a structured field of its results; it returns
      the token.

   Checks by others.  Between 08:25 and 08:33 UTC on 25 November 2026 an
   independent reader fetched all 716 statements and compared the digest
   of each with the published list. 715 matched.  One, fetched once,
   verified under the publisher's key and was on no list: the read of
   one input file had failed and the statement had been built and signed
   without its "limits" member.  The implementation now signs nothing
   when an input cannot be read, and the digest is listed as issued in
   error.  The requirements on incomplete inputs and on the state
   "issued in error" in this revision come from this result.

   Composition.  Two test Subjects, issued with the same key and
   labelled as tests, exercise the states.  For one of them, an agent-
   side record that referenced revision 1 was sealed by the operator of
   [I-D.dobson-ai-decision-receipt] and anchored in Bitcoin block 977556
   before revision 2 was issued.  Revision 2 carries no block hash, and
   its order against that record cannot be derived from the records.
   Revision 3 adds "not_before" naming a later block, so the record
   precedes revision 3 by heights alone.  An independent reader looked
   the block up on a node of their own, found it 143 blocks above the
   receipt's anchor, and confirmed that the published digest covers the
   block hash; the order of the record and revision 3 therefore rests on
   a chain validated by someone other than the publisher.  A third party
   wrote verdict records under [I-D.wright-verdict-record] over the
   three test cases and registered them with a Transparency Service
   whose policy refuses a statement that cites one not registered; a
   statement citing the unregistered revision 2 was refused.  Each
   record names the version of the digest list it read.

   Vantage.  The same third party reported that its checks from one
   network location received an HTTP 403 response for one common default
   client at the publisher's first service address, and answers for a
   client that looked like a browser.  The cause was a setting of the
   network service in front of that address, not of the implementation.
   The statements are now also served from the Issuer's origin, where
   the same client receives answers.  A comparison of the bytes served
   with the bytes published found the data files, including the



Yetis                      Expires 7 June 2027                 [Page 16]

Internet-Draft              Figure Statements              December 2026


   statements' sources and the digest list, served unchanged, and the
   HTML pages altered by the same network service.  Section 8.1 follows
   from this.

   Other publishers.  Two figures of another publisher, a statistical
   office in another jurisdiction and outside housing finance, were
   restated under this profile from responses captured as retrieved, as
   demonstrations.  The profile needed no change; the source digest is
   then of a data service's response as retrieved, not of a document the
   publisher keeps unchanged, which each statement states among its
   limits.

   Two observations from operating it bear on the design.

   Comparing consecutive Editions of two monthly reports of one agency
   found five values printed in one Edition and printed differently in
   the next, with no revision mark: three loan counts that moved by one
   or two, and two delinquency rates that moved by 0.06 percentage
   point.  The "source_revision" member and case 3 of Section 9 exist
   because of these.

   In one measured condition, a language model that was given a sentence
   ending in a token and asked to answer a user's question kept the
   token in 1 of 36 answers when it was attached to the number, and in
   none when it stood at the end of the sentence.  The recommendation in
   Section 10 to carry the token in a structured field follows from
   this.
