  Figure Statements: A SCITT Profile for Published Figures Given to AI
                                 Agents
                  draft-yetis-scitt-figure-receipts-00

Abstract

   Profiles of SCITT for AI agents record what an agent did: which tool
   it called, under whose authority, and what bytes came back.  They
   state, correctly, that they do not show whether those bytes were
   true.  This document profiles the layer underneath: a Signed
   Statement, issued by the publisher of a figure, that binds the figure
   to its source, period and population, and that is superseded when the
   publisher corrects the figure or when the original source prints a
   different value in a later edition.  It also defines a short token
   that travels with the figure through tool results and prose, so that
   a later reader can find the statement and learn whether the figure is
   still the one published.

   The profile makes a narrow claim.  It shows that a figure is the one
   a named publisher published, from a named source, and whether it has
   changed since.  It does not show that the figure is correct.

1.  Introduction

   An AI agent that answers a question with a statistic usually obtained
   the statistic from somewhere: a tool, a retrieved page, a data
   service.  Several current proposals make the agent's side of this
   exchange accountable.  A SCITT profile for agent action receipts
   [I-D.noa-scitt-ai-agent-receipt] records each governed action in a
   signed, hash-chained record.  An execution receipt [I-D.zambo-aer1]
   commits to the exact bytes a tool returned.  A measurement capsule
   [I-D.templeman-scitt-measurement-capsule] records what a third party
   observed against what a subject declared.

   Each of these is explicit about its boundary.  A receipt for an
   action shows that a record was issued by a key and has not been
   altered; it does not show that the agent's inputs were true.  A
   commitment to output bytes shows that the bytes match; it says
   nothing about the value inside them.  The SCITT architecture itself
   is as plain: registering a Statement only proves that it was produced
   by an Issuer [RFC9943].

   So a chain of agent receipts can be complete and verifiable while the
   number at the end of it is stale.  This happens in practice in two
   ways that the agent cannot see:

   *  The publisher corrects a figure.  Copies of the earlier value
      persist in caches, datasets and model outputs.

   *  The original source restates a figure.  Official statistical
      reports print the previous period beside the current one, and the
      restated value sometimes differs from the value first printed,
      without any mark.  Section 13 reports five such cases found in two
      monthly reports of one agency.

   This document defines a Figure Statement: a SCITT Signed Statement
   whose Issuer is the publisher of the figure and whose Subject is the
   figure.  It is the publisher's half of the record.  An agent-side
   receipt can then commit to output that carries a reference to a
   Figure Statement, and a Relying Party can check both halves.

   Defining payload formats is outside the charter of the SCITT Working
   Group.  This document is offered as an individual profile, in the
   same way as the agent profiles it complements.

2.  Terminology

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "SHOULD", "SHOULD NOT" and "MAY" in
   this document are to be interpreted as described in BCP 14 [RFC2119]
   [RFC8174] when, and only when, they appear in all capitals, as shown
   here.

   Artifact, Statement, Signed Statement, Issuer, Subject, Transparency
   Service, Receipt, Transparent Statement and Relying Party are used as
   defined in [RFC9943].  In this document "Receipt" always has that
   meaning.

   Figure:  A single published value together with what it measures: a
      quantity, a period, a population and a source.

   Publisher:  The party that publishes a Figure and issues the

      Figure Statement for it.  The Publisher may have computed the
      Figure or may be restating a Figure printed by someone else.

   Computed Figure:  A Figure the Publisher derived from a dataset by a
      stated method.

   Restated Figure:  A Figure the Publisher copied, without computation,
      from a document issued by another party (the Source).

   Edition:  One dated issue of a recurring Source document.

   Figure Statement:  The Statement defined in Section 3.

   Figure Token:  The short textual reference defined in Section 5.

3.  The Figure Statement

   A Figure Statement is a JSON object.  Before hashing or signing it is
   serialized with the JSON Canonicalization Scheme [RFC8785].  It has
   the following members.

   id (string, REQUIRED):  An identifier for the Figure, unique within
      the Publisher, made of lowercase letters, digits and hyphens.  It
      does not change when the value changes.

   value (string, REQUIRED):  The Figure exactly as it is displayed,
      including any unit sign and digit grouping.

   kind (string, REQUIRED):  "computed" or "restated".

   claim (object, REQUIRED):  The machine form of the Figure: at least a
      numeric value, a metric name and a period.  This is the object the
      Figure Token's digest is taken over (Section 5).

   source (object, REQUIRED):  For a Restated Figure: the title of the
      Source document, its Edition, the SHA-256 digest of the document
      as retrieved, the table and the row or cell the value was copied
      from, and any exclusion the Source states for that table.

      For a Computed Figure: the dataset and its version or digest, the
      definition of the population, the numerator and the denominator as
      counts where they exist, and a reference to the method.

   issued (string, REQUIRED):  The time of issue, as an [RFC3339]
      timestamp.

   supersedes (string, OPTIONAL):  The Figure Token of the
      Figure Statement this one replaces.

   source_revision (object, OPTIONAL):  For a Restated Figure only: the
      value the Source printed for the same period in a later Edition,
      and that Edition.  See Section 7.

   limits (array of strings, OPTIONAL):  Statements the Figure does not
      support, in the Publisher's words; for example that an aggregate
      rate is not a probability for an individual.

   A Publisher MUST NOT issue a Figure Statement of kind "restated" for
   a value it has adjusted, rounded differently from the Source, or
   derived from several cells.

4.  Use with SCITT

   A Figure Statement is carried as the payload of a Signed Statement
   [RFC9943], a COSE_Sign1 object [RFC9052], with these requirements in
   addition to those of the architecture.

   *  The Issuer (CWT Claims "iss") MUST be an HTTPS URI under the
      Publisher's control.

   *  The Subject (CWT Claims "sub") MUST be an HTTPS URI that contains
      the Figure's id and at which the current Figure Statement can be
      retrieved.

   *  The content type MUST be "application/json".  The payload MUST be
      the canonical serialization.

   When a Figure changes, for any of the reasons in Section 7, the
   Publisher MUST issue a new Signed Statement with the same Issuer and
   Subject, and SHOULD register it with every Transparency Service that
   holds the earlier one.  The earlier Signed Statement is not
   withdrawn.  This follows the architecture's guidance for an Issuer
   whose view of an Artifact changes.

   Registration matters here for a specific reason.  A Publisher that
   corrects a Figure quietly, replacing the value and leaving no trace,
   defeats every downstream check.  A Transparency Service that holds
   both statements, with Receipts, dates the change by a log the
   Publisher does not control.

5.  The Figure Token

   A Signed Statement is too large to sit beside a number in a sentence
   or in a tool result.  The Figure Token is a short reference that can.

Internet-Draft              Figure Statements              November 2026


    token   = open prefix ":" id ":" value ":" digest
              [ ":" channel ] close
    open    = %xE2.9F.A6   ; U+27E6, UTF-8 encoded
    close   = %xE2.9F.A7   ; U+27E7, UTF-8 encoded
    prefix  = 2*8ALPHA     ; chosen by the Publisher
    id      = 1*( %x61-7A / DIGIT / "-" )
    value   = 1*( %x21-39 / %x3B-7E )   ; as displayed; no ":", no space
    digest  = 8HEXDIG      ; lowercase
    channel = ALPHA 4DIGIT ; where and when this copy was issued

   The digest is the first eight hexadecimal characters of the SHA-256
   digest of the canonical serialization of the "claim" member.  The
   brackets were chosen because they do not occur in ordinary prose or
   in JSON syntax; a verifier SHOULD also accept a token whose brackets
   were lost in copying.

   The token is a pointer, not a proof.  Thirty-two bits do not resist a
   deliberate collision, and nothing in the token is signed.  A verifier
   MUST resolve the token to the Figure Statement and compare the full
   digest before reporting any state.

   A Publisher makes the following discoverable at a well-known location
   on the Issuer's origin: the prefix it uses, a URI template from id to
   Figure Statement, the digest rule and any earlier rule with its end
   date, and a list of every digest ever issued for each id.

6.  Verification

   Given a token, a verifier retrieves the current Figure Statement for
   the id and reports exactly one of these states.

   current:  The digest matches the current statement and the value in
      the token equals its "value" member.

   superseded:  The digest does not match the current statement but
      appears in the Publisher's list of issued digests for that id.
      The Figure was changed after this token was issued.  The verifier
      reports the current value.

   altered:  The digest matches the current statement but the value in
      the token does not.  The number beside a genuine reference was
      changed.

   unrecognized:  The id is unknown, or the digest was never issued for
      it.

   Independently of the state, if the current statement has a
   "source_revision" member the verifier MUST report it.  A token can be
   "current", because the Publisher's record of the first printing is
   unchanged, while the Source has since printed another value.

   Verification needs only the Publisher's public files.  A verifier
   SHOULD be able to run where the reader is, without sending the token
   to the Publisher (see Section 11).

7.  Changes to a Figure

   Three different events change what a careful reader should be told.
   They are kept apart because they have different authors.

   1.  The Publisher corrects its own Figure.  A new Figure Statement is
       issued with "supersedes".  Earlier tokens verify as "superseded".

   2.  The Source issues a marked revision.  For a Restated Figure the
       Publisher issues a statement for the revised value, citing the
       revising Edition, and adds "source_revision" to the statement for
       the first printing.

   3.  The Source prints a different value for the same period in a
       later Edition without marking it.  The Publisher treats it
       exactly as case 2.  The absence of a mark is recorded in the
       "source_revision" member, since the reader of the later Edition
       has no other way to learn that the value moved.

   In cases 2 and 3 the statement for the first printing remains true as
   a record of what that Edition printed, and its token remains
   "current".  A Publisher of Restated Figures therefore needs to read
   each new Edition against the last, for the periods both contain,
   before issuing statements for the new one.

8.  Use with Agent Receipts and Tool Protocols

   A tool that returns a Figure to an agent SHOULD return the
   Figure Token in a structured field of the result, in addition to any
   text.  Measurement reported in Section 13 found that a token placed
   in prose is dropped by a language model in nearly every restatement,
   so the text is not a reliable carrier.

   Where an agent-side record commits to the bytes of a tool result, as
   in [I-D.zambo-aer1] and [I-D.noa-scitt-ai-agent-receipt], and those
   bytes contain Figure Tokens, the two records compose without either
   format changing.  A Relying Party verifies the agent-side record to
   learn that these bytes were returned, and then verifies each token to
   learn whether the Figures in them are still the published ones.
   Neither check implies the other.

   An agent or its operator MAY re-verify tokens at the time an answer
   is shown, not only at the time the tool was called.  For long-running
   work and for stored memory the two times differ, and a Figure can be
   superseded between them.

9.  What a Figure Statement Does Not Establish

   *  That the Figure is correct.  A Publisher can compute wrongly, and
      a Source can print a wrong value.  The statement makes the
      Publisher accountable for what it published; it does not check it.

   *  That the population or the denominator is the right one for a
      reader's question.  They are stated so that the reader can decide.

   *  That a Restated Figure is the Source's latest value.  It is the
      value in the named Edition.

   *  Anything about a sentence built around the Figure.  A current
      token beside a number does not license a causal claim, a forecast
      or a statement about an individual.

   *  That the party presenting a token is the Publisher or is endorsed
      by it.

10.  Security Considerations

   The considerations of [RFC9943] apply.  The following are specific to
   this profile.

   Short digest.  The eight-character digest is an index.  An attacker
   can construct a different claim object with the same eight
   characters.  This yields nothing unless a verifier trusts the token
   alone, which Section 5 forbids.

   Borrowed tokens.  A genuine token can be placed beside an unrelated
   number or an unrelated sentence.  The "altered" state detects a
   changed value.  It does not detect a changed meaning; see Section 9.






Yetis                      Expires 28 May 2027                  [Page 8]

Internet-Draft              Figure Statements              November 2026


   Equivocation by the Publisher.  Without registration, a Publisher can
   serve different statements to different readers or rewrite its list
   of issued digests.  Registration with a Transparency Service, and
   Receipts, are what make the history checkable.

   Source documents.  The digest of a Source document shows which bytes
   the Publisher read.  If the Source replaces a document under the same
   name, the digest no longer matches the document a reader retrieves.
   Publishers SHOULD record a public archive capture of the bytes they
   read.

11.  Privacy Considerations

   A verification request tells the Publisher that someone holds a given
   Figure, and when.  A Publisher that offers a verification service
   SHOULD store no network address and no full user-agent string with
   such requests, and SHOULD publish what it does store.  Because
   verification needs only public files, a verifier can run in the
   reader's own software and make no request that names the token.

   A Figure Statement describes published aggregates.  It MUST NOT
   contain information about an identifiable person.

13.  Implementation Status

   This section is to be removed before publication as an RFC.  It
   follows [RFC7942].

   One implementation exists, operated by the author at
   financeratecalc.com.  As of the date of this document:

   *  716 Figures carry tokens: figures computed from the public United
      States Home Mortgage Disclosure Act record, and figures restated
      from monthly reports of the Department of Housing and Urban
      Development, Ginnie Mae and the Small Business Administration.

   *  The claim object, digest rule, token syntax, verification states
      and the well-known manifest are implemented.  Some member and
      state names differ from this document, which generalizes them (the
      implementation says "stale" where this document says "superseded",
      and "display_value" for "value").  A verification service, an in-
      browser verifier that uses only the public files, and a
      conformance suite that another publisher can run against its own
      site are public.

   *  A tool server returns each Figure with its token to AI agents.

   *  Not yet implemented: per-Figure COSE_Sign1 Signed Statements and
      registration with a SCITT Transparency Service.  At present a
      snapshot of every id, value and digest is signed and recorded in a
      public transparency log of another kind, which dates changes but
      does not yield Receipts as defined in [RFC9943].

   Two observations from operating it bear on the design.

   Comparing consecutive Editions of two monthly reports of one agency
   found five values printed in one Edition and printed differently in
   the next, with no revision mark: three loan counts that moved by one
   or two, and two delinquency rates that moved by 0.06 percentage
   point.  The "source_revision" member and case 3 of Section 7 exist
   because of these.

   In one measured condition, a language model that was given a sentence
   ending in a token and asked to answer a user's question kept the
   token in 1 of 36 answers when it was attached to the number, and in
   none when it stood at the end of the sentence.  The recommendation in
   Section 8 to carry the token in a structured field follows from this.
