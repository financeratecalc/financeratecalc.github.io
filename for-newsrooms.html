A figure that says when its source has changed

Agencies restate last month’s numbers in this month’s report, and the report does not mark the change. An article that quoted the first printing goes on saying it. A link or a small badge next to the number tells the reader, and any AI system that reads the page, whether it is still the figure the agency prints.

Where the receipts come from

  • Official monthly figures: HUD’s FHA production and loan performance reports, Ginnie Mae issuance, SBA approvals. Copied as printed, with the document’s hash, table and row. Each new edition is compared with the last; changes are in the revision ledger.
  • Denial rates from the federal HMDA record, such as the national FHA rate of for 2025, by state, metro and lender, each with its denominator.
  • Cite a finding gives the sentence, the receipt and the attribution ready to paste.

What a receipt shows, and what it does not

A receipt shows that the number is the one published, from the named document or file, and whether it has been corrected or restated since. It does not show that the agency’s number is right, and for a figure we compute it does not prove our denominator: that is stated in the claim document and can be recomputed from the public file. Every figure is a historical aggregate, not a forecast and not a judgement of any lender.
