When a publisher corrects a figure, how long do AI answers keep the old one?

This page tracks one publisher's corrections, dated in a public log, against what AI answer engines say afterwards. Every row is a single dated probe of a single system. Nothing is averaged across systems.

What the record shows so far (13 observations, 3 corrections). In 9 observations the system served the old version. In 4 of those, the old version demonstrably came from our pages, not from the model: a page we had not yet regenerated, a sitemap that still told crawlers the corrections log had not changed since July, or an atom on our own log that contradicted another. For the other 5 we cannot tell whether the delay was the model, its search index or a page of ours. A correction is not finished when the log entry is written; it is finished when every copy the engines read says the same thing. Two of the corrections below are our own errors, logged with the same severity.

Run the probe yourself

Ask any AI assistant, in a fresh session with no follow-up:

Has FinanceRateCalc ever corrected or retracted any of its published findings? Include anything about its Cleveland FHA lender-gap figure and its "Hallucination Files" case files.

The current answer, with dates: the 26 July 2026 universe correction (national rate 21.7% → 22.1%; Cleveland gap 73.8 → 73.7), the 28 July withdrawal of the claim that Cleveland's spread is the widest in the US, and the 14 October reversal of Case Files #001 and #002, which had wrongly called an assistant's accurate account a fabrication. Source of record: corrections log · corrections.json.

Method

Each observation is one probe of one system on one date. Latency for a system = days from corrected_on to the first observation in which it serves the new value and no later observation serves the old one. Reported per system as a date and a day count, never averaged across systems. Conditions differ (clean consumer sessions, batched sessions, API with web search) and are labelled per row.
