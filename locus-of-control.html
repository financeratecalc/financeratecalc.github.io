“71% of FHA mortgage denials are not the borrower’s fault” — withdrawn.

This page, published in June 2026, said that lender identity explained 63.4% of FHA denial variance, that 71% of denials were “outside the borrower’s control”, and that “routing optimization” addressed that 71%. It is withdrawn for three reasons, each already recorded elsewhere on this site:

  1. The number. The measured share of explained variation attributable to lender identity in the 2025 record is 37.97% (859,090 decisions), published with a receipt as The Door Effect — ⟦FRC:door-effect-38pct-2026:38%:c0f658fe⟧. The 63.4% and the derived 71% were corrected in the corrections log on 2026-09-02 and should not be cited.
  2. The causal language. “Not the borrower’s fault” and “outside the borrower’s control” are causal claims. HMDA aggregates cannot support them: variance between lenders reflects applicant mix, product and channel as well as underwriting. The current statement is associational only.
  3. The routing advice. “Routing optimization” told readers to pick lenders by observed rate. This site does not recommend lenders or predict applications; the lender tables are historical observations about institutions.

The lender values quoted on the old page (CrossCountry 6.37%, NewRez 53.27%) were also from before the 26 July correction; the receipted 2025 values are 6.2% and 52.8%. The URL is kept so that existing links and citations land on this notice rather than on a 404. The page’s earlier text remains in the repository history.
