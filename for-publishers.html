When an answer engine cites a statistic, the number usually survives. The sentence around it usually does not: the population, the period, the program scope, the attribution, and what the figure does not establish. We measured this on our own figures for six weeks, with a public instrument, and found the failure is mostly omission and drift, not invention. Accuracy monitoring does not see it. We can run the same instrument on yours.

What we found on ourselves (the credential). One frontier model, 12 questions, 3 repeats, with our data connected: the figure was right in every numeric answer and the full contract (figure + population + period + source, nothing added) was met in 1 to 11 of 36 answers depending on what we shipped. A web answer engine cited us as primary source and attributed a count to us that we never published. Another served our corrected figure without naming any source. A third served a figure we had corrected 58 days earlier, because one of our own pages still carried it. Everything above is published, including our own errors: the automated series, the misquote ledger, the corrections log.

Not SEO, not "AI visibility" ranking, not a promise that engines will cite you more.
