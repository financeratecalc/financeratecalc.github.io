Revision ledger: official figures that changed between editions

What this is. Monthly official reports restate earlier months. When the restated number differs from the one first printed, and the later edition does not mark it as revised, the change is easy to miss: a person or an AI system that read the first edition keeps quoting a number the publisher no longer prints. This page lists every such change found by comparing editions: 2 changed out of 138 figures compared so far.

What it does not say. Revising a figure is ordinary statistical practice; late records arrive and counts are updated. An entry here records only that two editions print different numbers for the same month and that the later one carries no revision mark for it. It says nothing about why, and it is not a finding of error. Figures compared and found unchanged are counted too, so the share that changes is visible.

How to use it

  • Quoting a figure: name the edition. “65,328 (July 2026 report; restated as 65,327 in the August 2026 report)” is complete; either number alone is not.
  • Checking a receipt: verify.html returns the publisher’s later value with any receipt whose figure was restated.
  • Machine-readable: data/revisions.json (this ledger), data/official/ (every edition as restated, with document hashes). Method: scripts/official_restate.py.
  • All restated figures: official-figures.html.
