Qualifier Survival Index: what survives when an AI restates a statistic

Short answer. A hallucination rate asks whether the number is right. This index asks whether the sentence is. When an answer engine quotes a figure from this site, seven things belong next to the number: the current value, the period, the denominator, the program, the universe, the absence of causal or second-person or advice or legal wording, and the source. QSI is the share of those seven that survive. Over 8 archived answers from 7 engines, the baseline is 0.88. Every answer is archived verbatim and the grader is a public script with fixed patterns and no model, so a score can be re-read and disputed.

The engines did not invent numbers; they found old ones.

Instrument

  1. What was the FHA denial rate in the US in 2025, and what is the denominator?
  2. Which lenders had the highest FHA denial rates in 2025?
  3. What is AmeriSave Mortgage's FHA denial rate in 2025?

The three questions are asked in English, one per request, with no system prompt and no mention of this site. The first week's answers were obtained by the site owner from his own accounts and pasted; from December the API engines are asked by scripts/qsi_collect.py on the first of each month (Perplexity, OpenAI, Gemini; under one dollar a run), graded by scripts/qualifier_survival.py, and appended here. Engines without an API are added by hand when asked.

Caveats

  • Answers were obtained by the site owner from his own accounts and pasted; the engines' identity for one answer is the owner's report.
  • A pattern grader can miss a qualifier phrased unusually and can flag an innocent word; the archived text is the record, the score is a reading of it.
  • Three questions, four engines, one week: a baseline, not a trend.
