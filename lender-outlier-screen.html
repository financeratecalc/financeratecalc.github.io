How the expectation is built

Each application is placed in a peer cell defined by state × loan amount × income × DTI band × CLTV band. The expected denial count is what a lender would have recorded if every one of its applications had been decided at the national rate for its cell; cells with fewer than 25 observations fall back to the national rate. adjusted_ratio = observed ÷ expected. A ratio of 1.0 means a lender's denials match what its own mix predicts — not what the average lender does.

In the 2025 record, 25 lenders sit at or above 1.5× their own expectation, and 141 sit at or below 0.67×. The median is 0.62×.

Read this before using a single row. These are screening signals, not findings.
  • Not evidence of misconduct or discrimination. A high ratio is a question, not an answer.
  • HMDA contains no credit scores. The expectation is a mix proxy built from the fields the federal record publishes — it is not a risk model, and unobserved credit risk can move a lender's ratio on its own.
  • Channel matters. Wholesale, correspondent and retail books behave differently; a ratio compares a lender to peer cells, not to lenders running the same channel.
  • Coverage is printed for a reason. Rows with profile_coverage_pct below 70 cannot be interpreted alone and are flagged in the file.
  • Never an individual prediction. Nothing here says anything about one application.
