What is assumed

The home price was chosen as an example for Charlotte when this page was first written in May 2026. It is not a current median and this page does not tie it to a dated dataset. The 6.7% rate is an assumption, not a quote. Property tax and insurance are round assumptions. The example leaves out mortgage insurance, association dues, closing costs and every other debt the household carries, any of which raises the real figure.

The 28 / 36 convention

A common rule of thumb puts housing costs near 28% of gross income and all debt payments near 36%. It is a convention; loan programs and underwriting systems apply their own limits and look at far more than this ratio. Under the assumptions above, a payment equal to 28% of this income corresponds to a home price of about $255,355, and 36% to about $334,623.

What the federal record says about North Carolina

Credit score rules, with their sources

FHA: a minimum decision credit score of 580 for maximum financing, and 500 to 579 with at least 10% down (HUD Mortgagee Letter 2010-29). Fannie Mae: no fixed minimum score for loans run through its automated underwriting system for casefiles created on or after 16 November 2025; the earlier floor was 620 (rule file with the issuer's documents). Individual lenders may set higher requirements of their own.
