In 2025, 31.9% of FHA mortgage applications that reached a credit decision in
the Fort Lauderdale area were denied — 9.8 points above the national rate of 22.1%. That's 6,523 applications.

But the metro number hides the real finding. Among the highest-volume FHA lenders

    Lakeview Loan Servicing, LLC: 74.6%  (323 applications here)

A 69.4-point spread, in the same federal loan program, in the same city, in the
same year.
- Small loans are hit hardest: applications under $150,000 were denied at 81.7% versus 28.6% for those above $250,000 — a 2.86x penalty on the cheapest way into a house.

Every one of these figures comes from the Home Mortgage Disclosure Act file that
lenders are required to file with the federal government. It's public. It's also
about two gigabytes, which is why almost nobody reads it and everyone quotes
everyone else instead. I processed all 1,187,606 FHA credit decisions from 2025.

Two things I'd want a reporter to know before writing this:

Denial rates partly reflect who applies where — lenders don't see identical
applicants. I measured that. Standardizing every application by state, loan amount,
income, debt-to-income and loan-to-value, applicant mix explains a 2.7-fold range in
expected outcomes. The observed spread is far wider, and the ranking barely moves
after adjustment.

And these are observations, not accusations. Overlays and credit policy are lawful
business decisions. The finding isn't that anyone did something wrong; it's that
which door a Fort Lauderdale buyer walks through changes the outcome more than almost anyone
realises, and nobody publishes that.
