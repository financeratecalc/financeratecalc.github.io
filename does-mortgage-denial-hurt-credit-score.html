Straight answer

Does a mortgage denial hurt your credit score? No.

Short answer

The denial itself is not reported to credit bureaus and does not appear on your credit report. The application's hard inquiry does — typically a few points, fading within months. And the next lender cannot see that you were refused. They see that you applied somewhere.

What actually happens on your report

A credit report records that an inquiry took place. It does not record the outcome. There is no field for "denied," no flag, no note. This surprises people because a denial feels like a permanent mark, and it isn't one.

What is recorded is the hard inquiry, which usually costs a small number of points and diminishes quickly. If you applied to several mortgage lenders within the shopping window, all of it counts as one inquiry. Why that window matters.

What does change — and it is not your score

Under federal law a lender that denies you must tell you why, in writing, within 30 days. That notice is the single most useful document you will receive, and most people file it away without reading it closely.

It matters because denial reasons are not distributed evenly across lenders. Across the 100 largest FHA lenders in 2025, the median shares of cited reasons were:

Cited reasonMedian share of denials
Debt-to-income40.3%
Credit history25.4%
Collateral / appraisal10.6%
Unverifiable information7.9%
Insufficient cash6.8%
Employment history5.0%
Incomplete application1.8%

Those medians hide something important. "Incomplete application" is 1.8% at the typical lender — and 75.2% at one large servicer. If that is the reason on your letter, it may say more about the door than about your file. The full finding.

The part that costs you, if you let it

Not the score. The assumption that the answer would be the same anywhere. In 2025 denial rates across the 100 largest FHA lenders ran from 1.8% to 78.7%, and in the typical metro the gap between the softest and hardest high-volume lender was 22.2 points. A refusal is one institution's decision on one day, recorded in a federal file alongside 1,187,606 others.
